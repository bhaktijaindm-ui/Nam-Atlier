The Lantern Facade

A Glowing Monolith of Rajasthan Sandstone and Perforated Screens
Location Udaipur, India
Year Completed 2024
Enclosed Area 16,000 SQ FT
Execution Duration 20 Months
Architectural Service Staircase, Foyer and Facade

“Commanding an elevated ridge overlooking the lake city of Udaipur, The Lantern Facade reinterprets regional stone jharokha traditions into a monumental contemporary residential envelope. Clad in hand-hewn cream sandstone panels and laser-chiseled stone screens that filter harsh desert sun, the facade transforms at dusk into a glowing architectural beacon reflected across a 30-meter water weir.”

The Brief

The patrons, scions of an esteemed Mewari industrial family, commissioned NAM Atelier to design a 16,000-square-foot luxury lakeside estate on a prominent rocky ridge in Udaipur. The site demanded an exterior facade of majestic presence—one that would honor the profound stone architectural heritage of Mewar's palaces and forts while creating a modern, energy-efficient living environment.

The architectural envelope needed to withstand blistering summer heat, fierce monsoon dust storms, and intense lakefront solar reflection, while presenting a dynamic, captivating visual presence from both land and water approaches.

The client also requested the inclusion of modern sustainability criteria: achieving net-zero passive cooling efficiency during transitional seasons and integrating comprehensive rainwater harvesting.

The Challenge

Udaipur's harsh desert climate subjects building facades to surface temperatures exceeding 52°C during summer, leading to severe thermal expansion and cooling loads if glazed carelessly. Mitigating direct solar gain without obstructing panoramic lake views was the primary environmental challenge.

Civilly, the facade’s heavy masonry elements—including twenty-four solid sandstone arches and sixty tons of perforated stone screens—had to be seismically anchored to a modern reinforced concrete structural skeleton without thermal bridging or visible mechanical fixings.

Lakefront wind shear was an additional critical factor, requiring aerodynamic facade wind-tunnel testing to ensure that the delicate perforated stone screens would not generate howling wind whistle during severe seasonal storms.

The Concept

Our concept, 'The Glowing Lantern', reinterprets the traditional Mewari jharokha (projecting oriel window) and jaali (perforated stone screen) through clean, monolithic contemporary geometry. We designed a dual-skin ventilated facade system: an inner thermal envelope of high-performance double glazing, shielded by an exterior skin of hand-chiseled sandstone screens.

By day, the stone screens act as a passive solar filter, cutting thermal heat by seventy percent while casting dynamic shadow tapestries across interior floors. At night, warm interior and architectural grazing lights transform the solid stone facade into a delicate, glowing lantern.

This dual-skin design creates an architectural rhythm that breathes with the desert climate, providing deep shade and coolness by day, and radiant warmth by night.

Space by Space

Arrival begins along a sweeping vehicular driveway paved in flamed basalt flagstones, culminating at a majestic seven-meter-high entrance colonnade crafted from solid blocks of Jaisalmer teak sandstone. Flanked by unlacquered brass lanterns, the massive timber-and-brass pivot door provides a ceremonial threshold into the estate.

Facing the lake, expansive living loggias are shielded by five-meter-tall operable sandstone jaali panels that slide on concealed stainless steel tracks. At the base of the facade, a 30-meter-wide black basalt reflecting pool acts as a thermal cooling basin, dropping ambient air temperatures before breezes enter the residence.

At the rooftop level, an open-air stargazing pavilion features cantilevered sandstone pergolas that frame views of the ancient City Palace across the lake.

Materials and Finishes

The entire facade was built using indigenous Rajasthan sandstone from sustainable local quarries. Stone blocks were individually selected for subtle teak-like grain veining and warm golden undertones, hand-dressed by master artisans using traditional chisels.

The perforated jaali screens were carved from solid 75mm-thick sandstone slabs, engineered to resist severe monsoon wind gusts. Metal fittings—including door portals, window sub-frames, and decorative lanterns—were forged in solid marine-grade brass and marine-grade stainless steel.

Basalt stone for the reflecting pool was flamed to a deep, non-reflective charcoal finish that absorbs heat and prevents unwanted water glare into the ground-floor living salons.

Light and Atmosphere

Facade lighting was developed in close collaboration with international architectural lighting designers. Miniature optical LED grazers operating at 2200K were concealed within the stone reveal pockets, washing the textured masonry with warm golden light while keeping the light sources completely invisible.

At twilight, the facade reflection shimmers across the lake and the private reflecting pool, creating an unforgettable golden mirage that bridges ancient palatial memory with contemporary luxury living.

Interior light spills softly through the perforated jaali screens onto the outer terraces, casting delicate geometric lace patterns of shadow across the stone pavement.

Craft and Execution

The facade masonry was constructed using dry-hung ventilated facade engineering, allowing ambient air to circulate between the outer sandstone skin and the inner waterproofed concrete walls, venting hot air upward before it can heat the interior living spaces.

Over forty traditional Rajasthani stone carvers worked on-site for twelve months under the supervision of NAM Atelier’s civil engineers, uniting ancestral hand craftsmanship with millimeter-precise laser survey alignment.

Every stone bracket was anchored using seismic-rated 316 stainless steel expansion bolts with vibration-dampening neoprene washers, ensuring that the heavy stone facade can withstand tremors without mortar cracking.

The Result

The Lantern Facade stands as an iconic contemporary landmark in Udaipur. It honors the noble tradition of Rajasthan stone architecture while establishing a new benchmark in sustainable, climate-responsive luxury residential engineering.

It represents the crowning achievement of NAM Atelier’s portfolio—a timeless estate built to endure with grace, dignity, and beauty for generations to come.

For the family, it is a magnificent ancestral sanctuary that anchors their heritage in the sacred landscape of Udaipur with effortless modern majesty.
