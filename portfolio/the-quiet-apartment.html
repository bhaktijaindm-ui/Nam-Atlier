Penthouse and Apartment Design

The Quiet Apartment

Japanese Wabi-Sabi Aesthetics Infused with Indian Craftsmanship
Location Pune, India
Year Completed 2025
Enclosed Area 4,200 SQ FT
Execution Duration 11 Months
Architectural Service Penthouse and Apartment Design

“Located in the verdant Koregaon Park enclave of Pune, The Quiet Apartment is a 4,200-square-foot residential retreat conceived for an author and minimalist art patron. Guided by principles of restraint, natural light diffusion, and handcrafted joinery, the apartment rejects ostentation in favor of deeply meditative tactile harmony.”

The Brief

The client, a celebrated novelist and essayist, sought to downsize from an expansive suburban estate into a refined four-bedroom floor plate overlooking the ancient banyan trees of Koregaon Park, Pune. The design mandate was radical in its restraint: eliminate decorative excess, conceal electronic clutter, and craft a deeply contemplative environment prioritizing silence, natural materials, and ergonomics for reading and writing.

The space needed to effortlessly hold an extensive private library of over four thousand volumes while providing hospitable living spaces for intimate dinner discussions with writers, artists, and family members.

The client requested that no synthetic veneers, plastic paints, or high-gloss laminates be used anywhere in the construction, requiring all surfaces to be organic, breathable, and capable of developing a gentle patina over years of use.

The Challenge

The base developer floor plate suffered from low beam clearances, awkward structural columns that fractured the living zone, and noisy mechanical air conditioning provisions that disrupted the client's creative focus.

Our engineering challenge was to integrate a whisper-quiet variable refrigerant flow climate system within drop ceilings restricted to less than 150 millimeters, while re-sculpting the irregular structural columns into harmonious architectural pilasters clad in hand-applied clay plaster.

Acoustic isolation was also paramount: street sounds from surrounding avenues and internal plumbing noise from neighboring apartments had to be systematically neutralized without adding thick, heavy masonry walls that would crowd the floor plate.

The Concept

The concept draws inspiration from Japanese wabi-sabi philosophy—celebrating impermanence, honest textures, and organic simplicity—reinterpreted through indigenous Indian materials. We designed a continuous spatial flow where rooms glide into one another via full-height pocket doors, eliminating visual thresholds and unnecessary doors.

Rather than applying decorative wallpaper or glossy paints, we treated walls as soft canvas backdrops using natural breathable clay plaster that changes tone subtly from morning cream to late-afternoon amber.

The interior functions as a temple of quietude: unnecessary trim was eliminated, shadow gaps replaced conventional baseboards, and furniture was designed low to the ground to enhance the perception of vertical volume.

Space by Space

Upon entering, guests step onto a sunken genkan-inspired entry mat crafted from honed green Kota limestone, transitioning smoothly onto wide-plank white oak flooring. The living room features low-slung bespoke seating upholstered in natural oatmeal linen, oriented toward the green canopy outside.

The central spine of the apartment houses the library: continuous floor-to-ceiling oak bookcases with integrated reading alcoves, ladder rails, and concealed acoustic insulation. The writing study is oriented toward the quietest north elevation, featuring a solid oak trestle desk crafted without a single visible metal fastener.

The tea room and meditation corner features traditional tatami rush matting, flanked by sliding shoji-inspired screens made from handmade washi paper that diffuse daylight into an ethereal milky radiance.

Materials and Finishes

The palette is strictly tactile, non-toxic, and natural. Solid European white oak was treated with matte hardwax oil to preserve the raw, velvety grain of the wood without synthetic varnishes. Regional Kota stone was specified in a velvet-honed finish for wet areas and the entry foyer.

Drapery consists of double-weave Belgian linen left unlined to allow the gentle silhouettes of exterior branches to filter through into the living spaces. Hardware throughout features satin-brushed nickel with subtle knurling that provides satisfying tactile feedback upon use.

Walls were hand-plastered with a three-layer composite of unbaked river clay, finely ground wheat straw, and marble sand, creating an electrostatic-neutral wall surface that repels dust and purifies interior air.

Light and Atmosphere

Illumination is deliberate and poetic. During the day, deep sheer drapery softens intense tropical daylight into a tranquil, even glow. Direct downlights were completely eliminated from bedrooms and the study, replaced with concealed cove illumination and low-level task lamps with parchment and washi paper shades.

At dusk, warm 2400K architectural grazing reveals the subtle hand-troweled texture of the clay walls, creating an atmosphere reminiscent of twilight in a traditional Kyoto teahouse.

Lighting controls were programmed with dedicated reading, writing, and contemplation presets, allowing the author to transition the mood of the apartment with a single understated brass toggle switch.

Craft and Execution

The joinery throughout was executed by master carpenters trained in Japanese timber framing techniques. Bookcases feature hand-cut mortise and tenon joints with flush finger pulls carved directly into the drawer fronts.

Acoustic isolation was prioritized: all internal partition walls were constructed with dual drywall skins mounted on resilient acoustic isolation clips and packed with high-density mineral wool, preventing internal sound transmission and sealing the apartment from city noise.

All mechanical air supply plenums were lined with acoustic micro-perforated damping baffles, lowering operating sound levels to an imperceptible twenty-two decibels.

The Result

The Quiet Apartment demonstrates that ultimate luxury lies not in ornamentation, but in spatial clarity, acoustic tranquility, and material authenticity. It is an apartment that invites its inhabitants to slow down, breathe deeply, and reconnect with their creative essence.

It represents a seminal project in NAM Atelier's portfolio, proving that minimalist design can feel profoundly warm, human, and enduring.

For the author who inhabits it, the home has become an indispensable creative sanctuary—a quiet haven where words come naturally and the mind finds undisturbed rest.
