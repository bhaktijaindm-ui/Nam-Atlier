Penthouse and Apartment Design

Skyline Penthouse

Suspended High Above the Arabian Sea in Sculpted Bronze and Travertine
Location Mumbai, India
Year Completed 2024
Enclosed Area 6,800 SQ FT
Execution Duration 14 Months
Architectural Service Penthouse and Apartment Design

“Commanding panoramic views of Mumbai's southern coastline, the Skyline Penthouse spans two interconnected floors atop an iconic residential skyscraper. Stripped of developer-grade finishes, the shell was transformed into an ethereal sanctuary of warm minimalism featuring a double-height salon, floating bronze stair, and acoustic fluted walnut paneling.”

The Brief

The client, an international venture financier with homes in London and Singapore, acquired two raw duplex shells on the 54th and 55th floors of a newly erected tower in Worli, Mumbai. The brief called for a total spatial overhaul: creating a sophisticated urban retreat designed for entertaining global tastemakers while functioning as a quiet, deeply restorative sanctuary between demanding travel schedules.

Essential programmatic requirements included a dramatic double-height salon, a temperature-controlled 600-bottle wine cellar, an executive home office, two guest suites, and an expansive private master aerie occupying the entire upper tier.

Having lived in standard luxury apartments burdened with shiny gold trims and fake marble composites, the client stressed a vision of understated architectural integrity: heavy natural textures, muted tones, and museum-grade finishes that would frame the dramatic seascape without visual noise.

The Challenge

Sky-high construction in maritime Mumbai presents intense engineering parameters: extreme wind loads causing micro-sway in skyscraper structures, relentless coastal salt-spray humidity, and severe weight limitations on floor slabs that strictly precluded heavy wet-masonry partitions.

Moreover, all oversized structural elements—including five-meter-long steel stringers for the staircase and three-meter-wide stone slabs—had to be hoisted via exterior mast climbers in high wind conditions, requiring minute logistical synchronization with the building management.

Acoustically, the sheer altitude created whistling wind vortexes against the curtain wall during monsoon storms, demanding a secondary interior acoustic barrier system to maintain whisper-quiet tranquility within the master living chambers.

The Concept

Our conceptual framework, 'Suspended Monolith', focused on anchoring the floating sensation of high-altitude living with grounded, heavy architectural materials. Rather than filling the space with typical luxury condominium clutter, we designed clean architectural interventions: a monolithic travertine fireplace block, a cantilevered bronze-and-oak staircase, and seamless acoustic ceiling planes.

Sightlines were sculpted to frame the changing moods of the Arabian Sea horizon, allowing dawn fog, saturated sunset golds, and the nocturnal glitter of Mumbai's marine drive to become the primary dynamic artwork of the interior.

We utilized a muted tonal palette inspired by the meeting of sea and limestone cliffs, creating an atmosphere that feels expansive, weightless, and profoundly protective.

Space by Space

Entry via a private elevator foyer opens directly into a compressed bronze-lined vestibule that intensifies the dramatic reveal of the double-height salon beyond. In this seven-meter-high volume, low-profile custom bouclé seating and an expansive carved travertine table keep vertical sightlines unblocked toward the sea.

Behind the main salon, a discreet pocketing door opens to an intimate whisky lounge and executive boardroom wrapped in smoked oak acoustic paneling. Adjacent, an open dry kitchen features a monolithic travertine island that doubles as an evening cocktail bar during dinner parties.

Upstairs, the primary bedroom suite floats like a private glass pavilion, complemented by a spa bathroom carved entirely from honed travertine slabs, complete with a freestanding stone tub positioned against floor-to-ceiling glass overlooking the coastal horizon.

Materials and Finishes

The tactile palette celebrates muted tonal harmony over flashy contrast. Italian Navona travertine was selected for its creamy beige warmth and cut cross-grain to reveal subtle linear sedimentary striations. All vertical surfaces in the double-height space receive micro-cement coatings that absorb light without reflection.

Metalwork throughout utilizes solid architectural bronze with a custom chemical patina that deepens with age, sealed with microcrystalline wax. Upholstery features heavy textured bouclé wool, pure cashmere drapery, and supple saddle leather that soften the hard architectural geometry.

Flooring transitions seamlessly from large-format honed travertine in the salon to wire-brushed smoked European oak in the upper sleeping quarters, imparting warmth and barefoot softness.

Light and Atmosphere

Because high-rise residences are flooded with intense daylight throughout the day, architectural solar-control membranes were applied to all exterior glass to block ninety-nine percent of ultraviolet rays and forty percent of thermal heat without tinting coastal views.

Interior illumination follows an architectural philosophy of low-level pools of light. Plaster-in trimless fixtures produce zero glare, while hidden linear grazing channels accent the fluted oak walls and bronze staircase stringers, transforming the penthouse into an atmospheric, glow-infused observatory after dark.

At twilight, the automated lighting system activates a candle-like 2200K ambient wash that matches the setting sun, making the transition from day to evening feel organic and restorative.

Craft and Execution

The centerpiece cantilevered staircase was pre-engineered in aerospace-grade structural steel and welded on-site with laser-guided precision, achieving an entirely floating tread system with zero floor-to-ceiling intermediate supports.

To insulate the penthouse against tower vibration and external wind whistle, all exterior window frames were retrofitted with specialized silicone acoustic gaskets and triple-layer laminated glass, lowering interior ambient sound levels to thirty-two decibels—whisper-quiet tranquility fifty stories above the bustling metropolis.

Every stone slab was reinforced on its underside with high-modulus carbon-fiber backing mesh, reducing dead load by thirty percent while preventing risk of seismic cracking.

The Result

The Skyline Penthouse stands as one of Mumbai's premier examples of restrained luxury interior architecture. By stripping away extraneous ornament and emphasizing proportion, natural material texture, and light, the residence achieves a timeless, museum-grade calm.

It is a masterclass in penthouse design—commanding, serene, and built to the highest international standards of luxury civil contracting.

It proves that even in the dense urban verticality of Mumbai, home can be an untouchable aerie of contemplation, beauty, and absolute silence.
