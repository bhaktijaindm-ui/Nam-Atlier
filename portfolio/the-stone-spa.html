Bathroom and Spa

The Stone Spa

Monolithic Mineral Serenity in Honed Travertine and Teak
Location Mumbai, India
Year Completed 2024
Enclosed Area 1,600 SQ FT
Execution Duration 7 Months
Architectural Service Bathroom and Spa

“Conceived as an elemental hydrothermal sanctuary in a coastal Mumbai penthouse, The Stone Spa rejects cosmetic tilework in favor of monolithic masonry. Carved from massive slabs of Navona travertine and anchored by a hand-carved solid basalt soaking tub, steam suite, and experiential rain shower, the space brings ancient thermal bathhouse rituals into the modern home.”

The Brief

The client, a wellness advocate and technology entrepreneur in Mumbai, commissioned NAM Atelier to transform a conventional master bathroom suite into an expansive 1,600-square-foot personal hydrothermal sanctuary. Inspired by ancient Roman thermae and Japanese onsen rituals, the brief demanded an unhurried, elemental experience prioritizing hydrotherapy, aromatherapy, and sensory detox.

Essential programmatic requirements included a massive hand-carved soaking tub capable of dual occupancy, an integrated Turkish steam hammam with aromatherapy vapor injectors, a dual rain-waterfall sensory shower, and private his-and-hers vanity zones.

The patron stressed that the bathroom should feel entirely distinct from commercial hotel aesthetics: no high-gloss tiles, no chrome-plated hardware, and no synthetic vanity units—only heavy, tactile, natural materials that breathe and age with dignity.

The Challenge

High-rise construction in humid coastal Mumbai presents extreme waterproofing and structural load challenges. Introducing a solid stone bathtub weighing nearly 1,400 kilograms along with hundreds of square meters of full-height natural stone slabs required structural beam reinforcement and zero-deflection floor leveling.

Furthermore, managing high-humidity steam environments adjacent to air-conditioned bedroom quarters required absolute vapor isolation, airtight door transoms, and negative-pressure continuous exhaust systems to prevent moisture migration into adjacent timber wardrobes.

Acoustic isolation was also critical: the powerful pumps for the sensory shower and steam generators had to operate silently so as not to disrupt the tranquility of the adjacent master sleeping quarters.

The Concept

Our architectural concept, 'The Mineral Grotto', strips away decorative ornamentation, treating the entire bathroom as if carved from a single continuous geological formation. We eliminated typical tile joints, grout lines, and plastic shower trays, composing the room with massive, book-matched slabs of honed Navona travertine and flamed black granite.

Water is celebrated as an architectural element: concealed spouts pour water in silent sheets from stone walls, while floor-level infinity troughs drain water seamlessly along wall perimeters without visible metal grates.

The experience is sequenced chronologically: moving from the warm vanity dry zone, through the rain portals, into the misty steam hammam, and culminating at the deep basalt soaking tub overlooking the city.

Space by Space

Entry through smoked bronze glass doors reveals the central soaking pavilion. Positioned before a full-height window overlooking the Mumbai skyline, a freestanding bathtub sculpted from a single boulder of black volcanic basalt commands the room. Flanking it, double vanity consoles in fluted travertine feature undermounted stone basins carved directly into the countertops.

Behind a continuous frameless glass screen lies the wet zone: an open rain shower with dual ceiling-flush nozzles and an integrated steam hammam featuring floating heated stone benches and chromotherapy lighting channels.

A private outdoor plunge terrace accessible via sliding stone screens offers a cool-air relaxation deck with teak daybeds, sheltered from coastal winds by fluted glass balustrades.

Materials and Finishes

The primary wall and floor surface is Italian Navona travertine, cross-cut and honed to a velvet-matte finish that provides natural slip resistance when wet. The soaking tub was hand-carved from Indonesian river basalt, its outer skin left rough-chiseled while the interior was polished to a silky smoothness.

Wet-area seating and duckboards utilize naturally water-resistant Burmese teak saturated with marine-grade botanical oils. All tapware and hardware were custom-machined from solid unlacquered brushed brass, engineered without chrome plating to prevent peeling under steam exposure.

Flooring in the wet zones incorporates flamed black granite flagstones with brushed textures, delivering superior grip under wet barefoot conditions.

Light and Atmosphere

Lighting was engineered as an emotional restorative layer. Direct overhead glare was strictly avoided; illumination is delivered via concealed LED troughs beneath the vanity plinths, recessed wall niches, and halo illumination behind circular bronze mirrors.

In the steam hammam, water-resistant fiber-optic ceiling points create a gentle celestial constellation effect, while warm 2200K cove grazing transforms the texture of the wet stone into a shimmering, sensual landscape during evening baths.

Natural daylight enters through acoustic low-E glass fitted with motorized teak louvers, allowing the bather to modulate the interplay of morning sun and shadow with a single bedside tap.

Craft and Execution

Civil execution required a multi-layered waterproof membrane comprising four coats of elastomeric polyurethane backed with fiberglass mesh, water-tested under flood conditions for fourteen consecutive days prior to stone cladding. Travertine slabs were mechanically anchored with marine-grade 316 stainless steel bracketry.

The steam generator and circulation pumps were housed in an external utility shaft with acoustic dampening, while dual commercial inline exhaust fans evacuate 900 CFM of moisture within two minutes of steam cycle completion.

The basalt soaking tub required specialized crane rigging to hoist the 1.4-ton monolithic stone block through a temporary exterior facade opening fifty floors above Mumbai's streets.

The Result

The Stone Spa represents the pinnacle of residential hydrothermal architecture. By prioritizing monolithic mineral textures, spatial clarity, and contemplative lighting, NAM Atelier delivered an enduring sanctuary where everyday cleansing is elevated into an exquisite wellness ritual.

It exemplifies the studio’s technical mastery in executing complex, heavy-civil wet environments with serene, effortless beauty.

The patron describes the bathroom as a life-altering wellness asset—a daily retreat that restores energy, focus, and physical peace amidst the relentless pace of urban Mumbai.
